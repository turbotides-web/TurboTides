<p>By Dr. Xuwen Qiu<br>
November 20, 2026</p>

<p>HEFEI, China, November 20, 2026 — Dr. Xuwen Qiu today issued the following statement regarding the jury verdict in the U.S. District Court for the District of Vermont in the case brought by Concepts NREC.</p>

<p>On November 12, 2026, a federal jury rejected Concepts NREC’s core intellectual-property claims against Dr. Xuwen Qiu and TurboTides Inc. The jury found no copyright infringement by Dr. Qiu or TurboTides Inc., and no trade secret misappropriation by Dr. Qiu. After careful consideration, the jury rejected claims by Concepts NREC that TurboTides software is a copy of Concepts software containing Concepts NREC’s proprietary technology.</p>

<p>The verdict was not a complete victory. The jury did find that Dr. Qiu breached his employment agreement. The verdict does not specify which contract provision was breached. The jury awarded no compensatory damages on the contract claim but assessed a punitive award that is less than 2% of what Concepts NREC demanded.</p>

<p>The only trade-secret liability finding was against TurboTides, Inc., not Dr. Qiu personally. That finding likely concerned an email written by a former employee of both companies containing a sales document for the competitive analysis of turbomachinery software market. It did not concern the TurboTides software itself, and it did not concern Dr. Qiu’s personal conduct.</p>

<p>Concepts NREC’s public statement characterizes the verdict as a finding that Dr. Qiu “developed a competing software program called TurboTides” while employed at Concepts. The jury did not make a specific finding on that issue. The fact is that TurboTides software took more than three years and more than 20 highly skilled developers in various fields to become a mature software product. The jury expressly rejected the copyright and personal trade-secret claims that would have supported that characterization.</p>

<blockquote>
<p>“I’m glad that the jury concluded that neither I nor TurboTides, Inc. infringed Concepts NREC copyright,” said Dr. Xuwen Qiu. “This 6-year-long case is Concepts NREC’s vengeful campaign against a former employee who had a dream to pursue a better technology. In my view, this case was never about protecting legitimate intellectual property; it was an attempt to crush a competitor. TurboTides competes through technology innovation, not by sitting on the technology of last century and trying to claim ownership of a product that one never developed. I’m excited to announce that we will soon release our ‘AI Turbo Engineer’ -- a brand-new AI-native agent, to help our worldwide customers make their turbomachinery designs better and faster.”</p>
</blockquote>

<p>TurboTides remains committed to providing advanced turbomachinery design software and support to its customers worldwide. We thank our customers, employees, families, and legal team for their continued trust and confidence.</p>

<p>Because post-trial proceedings are pending, we will not comment further on the specifics of the litigation beyond this statement.</p>
